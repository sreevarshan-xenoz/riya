# Riya v0.9: "Little Riya" (a desktop pet that lives with your agents)

Design document. 2026-10-20. Status: approved on autopilot.

The user pointed to thatpet.app: "i want like that my own will get riya like that".

## 1. Intent

ThatPet is a small animated character that lives on the desktop and reacts to the user's coding agents as they work.
- A bubble shows what the agent is doing right now: editing, running, searching.
- She thinks when the user sends a prompt and waves when an approval is needed.
- Confetti goes off when a turn finishes.
- Clicking her jumps to the agent that needs the user.

Riya already has most of the plumbing:
- Claude Code hook events (v0.4).
- Agent status.
- A buddy display mode with expressions, blinking, breathing and leaning.
- Click-through and dragging.

She doesn't move around, react to agent activity moment by moment, celebrate, or take the user to the agent.

v0.9 makes buddy mode a living desktop pet called "Little Riya". She walks, idles, sits, sleeps, can be picked up and dropped, and reacts to every agent step. All of it stays Riya: her art, her voice, her manners and her privacy rules. Nothing is copied from ThatPet's characters, art or name.

## 2. Goals

1. **Pet behaviour (buddy mode).** A small state machine runs in the body:
   - **idle:** breathing and blinking, with occasional fidgets.
   - **walk:** she strolls along the bottom of the work area, just above the taskbar. A walk is a 3–10 s burst every 40–180 s, at 60–90 DIP/s, facing her direction.
   - **sit:** after a walk or at random.
   - **sleep:** when the user is away, in quiet hours, or after 10 minutes of no activity.
   - **drag:** the user picks her up. She dangles and shows a "flustered" or "excited" expression.
   - **fall:** dropped above the floor, she falls with gravity, lands with a squash, then stands.
   - **react:** a short animation played for an event (§2.2).

   **When she stays put:**
   - She never walks while the user is focused on work, gaming, or in a full-screen app. She sits instead.
   - "Reduced motion" in settings, or the Windows reduced-motion preference, keeps her still. Reactions still show.

   **Motion and CPU:**
   - Walking uses procedural motion (step bob, slight sway, flip by direction) on her existing sprites.
   - If an art pack supplies `motions.walk` frames, those are used instead.
   - CPU stays at or below 3% while walking and at or below 1% while idle on the user's laptop.
   - The window moves at 20 Hz during walks and not at all otherwise.
2. **Agent-activity reactions.** These come from Claude Code hooks. v0.9 adds `PreToolUse` to the installer's events. Applying the installer is still the user's step.

   | Agent event | What she does |
   |---|---|
   | PreToolUse | An activity bubble with a short label: "editing engine.py", "running tests", "reading the spec", "searching the code", "browsing", "asking a helper". The label comes from the tool name and a safe summary of the input: a file basename, the first word of a command, never full arguments, passed through `redact`. |
   | UserPromptSubmit | A "thinking" pose. |
   | PermissionRequest | She waves and shows "needs you!" with the tool summary. |
   | Stop | A happy pose with a small confetti burst. |
   | Tool failure | An "oops" pose: worried, with a sweat drop. |
   | StopFailure | A sad pose. |
   | SubagentStop | A tiny "helper done" sparkle. |

   With several sessions, she shows the most recent activity. The badge counts the sessions that need the user. Bubbles are short-lived, at most 1 every 1.5 s per session, and the latest one wins.

   Spoken reports and their manners from v0.4 are unchanged. These are visual micro-reactions, never spoken, so they never interrupt focus.
3. **Click to jump.**
   - Clicking Little Riya while a session needs the user brings that session's terminal window to the front.
   - The core identifies the window from the hook request's TCP peer process: `GetExtendedTcpTable` gives the owning PID. It then walks the parent chain to the first process with a visible top-level window (Windows Terminal, conhost, VS Code, and so on) and calls `SetForegroundWindow`. Riya is foreground at that moment because the user just clicked her, so Windows allows it.
   - If no window is found, she opens the agents list instead.
   - Right-click opens her menu: display mode, don't look, reduced motion, "go to sleep", settings, quit.
4. **Celebrations and moments.**
   - Confetti, sparkles and the sweat drop are drawn on a small canvas inside her window, which gets 24 DIP of headroom for effects.
   - A rare surprise (1 in 50 finishes) is a little spin or heart, so it stays fresh.
5. **Size and placement.**
   - "Little" is the default buddy scale at 0.6, with 0.4–1.0 selectable in settings.
   - She walks only on the monitor she's on.
   - She remembers her spot when she's sitting or sleeping.
   - She never covers the taskbar or blocks clicks outside her own pixels, using v0.1 hit-testing.

## 3. Non-goals and anti-goals

**Non-goals:**
- Physics on other windows' title bars (a later idea).
- Multiple pets at once.
- A character store or paid unlocks.
- Supporting agents other than Claude Code, which can be added later through the same hook adapter.
- macOS or Linux.

**Anti-goals:**
- She distracts during focus: walking or big reactions while the user is focused, gaming or full-screen. Activity bubbles stay small and stay within her own window.
- Bubbles leak secrets or long text. Only basenames and first words appear, redacted and at most 40 characters.
- Click-to-jump focuses the wrong app, or focuses anything without a click.
- Walking burns CPU or GPU. The budgets in §2.1 are tested.
- Hooks slow Claude Code. The receiver keeps its fast 200 for PreToolUse too.

## 4. Design notes

**Core (`riya/agents/`).** The hub gains a per-session `activity {label, tool, at}` from PreToolUse. A new `activity` event type goes to the engine, and from there as a new core-to-body message: `agent_activity {session, label, kind, at}`, where kind is one of edit, run, read, search, web, delegate, think, wave, done, fail, oops or sparkle. It is rate-limited per session.

**`riya/agents/peer.py` (Windows).**
- `owner_pid(local_port, remote_port)` uses `GetExtendedTcpTable`.
- `window_for_pid(pid)` walks parents through `CreateToolhelp32Snapshot` and finds a visible top-level window.
- `focus(hwnd)`.
- The receiver records the peer PID per session when a hook arrives.
- Body to core: `focus_agent {id}`, sent only from a click. The core focuses that session's window or replies "not found".

**Body (`body/src/pet-logic.js`, pure and tested).**
- The pet state machine with seeded random timers.
- Walk and fall kinematics.
- Bounds: the work area and monitor from `screen.getDisplayMatching`.
- The reaction queue.
- `body/renderer.js` and `main.js` wire it in. Main moves the window during walks and falls, and the renderer draws effects.

**Art.**
- The procedural motion works with today's sprites.
- `sprite-manifest.schema.json` gains an optional `motions: {walk: [frame paths], sit: …, sleep: …}`.
- The Colab sprite runbook gains an optional walk-cycle section, which is the user's step.

**Settings.**
- `pet_enabled` (default true in buddy mode).
- `pet_scale` (0.6).
- `reduced_motion` (follows the OS by default).
- `pet_walks_when` (`breaks_only` by default, `always_but_focus` as the other option).

### 4.1 Builder-check decisions (my calls, binding)

1. **Who focuses the window.**
   - The core doesn't focus anything itself, because Windows foreground rules wouldn't let it.
   - On a click:
     1. The body activates its own window (`win.focus()`), so it holds foreground rights.
     2. It sends `focus_agent {id}`.
     3. At click time, the core resolves a target window. It returns the hwnd in `agent_window {id, hwnd | null}`.
     4. The body's main process spawns the short-lived helper `python -m riya.agents.peer focus <hwnd>`. Because the foreground process started it, Windows allows the switch.
   - The helper calls `ShowWindow(SW_RESTORE)` when the window is minimised, then `SetForegroundWindow`.
   - The hwnd is looked up at click time, never at hook time.
2. **Which window.**
   - Among the visible top-level windows of the resolved app process, pick the one whose title contains the session's cwd folder name.
   - If no title matches, fall back to that process's most recently active window.
   - Tabs can't be selected. Spec and UI promise "her terminal app", not a tab.
3. **Size.**
   - The buddy window is sized from `pet_scale`:
     - width = max(sprite width × scale, 240 DIP for the bubble);
     - height = sprite height × scale + 80 DIP of bubble room + 24 DIP of effects headroom.
   - Every move during a walk uses `setBounds` with that fixed width and height, which avoids fractional-DPI drift.
   - The floor puts the sprite's feet on `workArea.bottom`. With an auto-hide taskbar, that's the screen bottom; the doc notes this.
   - CPU is measured by `evaluation/pet_cpu.py`.
4. **When she walks and sleeps.**
   - The body's main process reads user idle from `powerMonitor.getSystemIdleTime()`. Gaming, full screen and away come from the core's presence, through a new on-change `presence {state}` message.
   - The default `pet_walks_when = "idle"`: she walks once the user has been idle for 30 s or more, and not while gaming, in full screen or during quiet hours.
   - She sleeps when presence is "away".
   - Any agent event wakes her to sit or react. Agent activity never makes her walk.
   - Reduced motion: the renderer reads `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)')` and reports it to main over IPC.
5. **Peer PID lookup.**
   - It runs synchronously inside the hook request handler, using `GetExtendedTcpTable` (IPv4, `TCP_TABLE_OWNER_PID_ALL`). The match is local port 8766 with remote port equal to `request.client.port`.
   - The PID is cached per session and looked up again only on a miss.
   - If the PID is 0 or unknown, or the call fails, the focus target is "not found" and she opens the agents list.

## 5. Verification
- **Pure tests:**
  - Pet state machine transitions.
  - Walk timing and bounds: she never leaves the work area and never covers the taskbar.
  - Fall kinematics and landing.
  - Reduced motion.
  - The focus, gaming and full-screen rules.
  - Reaction mapping per hook event.
  - Rate limiting.
  - Label summarising and redaction.
  - Bubble length at most 40.
- **Core tests:**
  - PreToolUse goes to `agent_activity`.
  - The installer adds PreToolUse, with temp paths only. The user's real settings are never touched.
  - The peer-PID map uses fakes.
  - `focus_agent` only from a body click.
- **CPU check:** `evaluation/pet_cpu.py` measures the body's CPU over 60 s each of idle and forced walk. It's a manual check.
- **Live check:** `evaluation/pet_check.py` POSTs a scripted hook sequence to a running core (prompt, PreToolUse edit, run, PermissionRequest, Stop) and prints the `agent_activity` messages seen. It never goes through real Claude Code hooks.
